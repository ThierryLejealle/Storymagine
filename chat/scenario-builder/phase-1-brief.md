# Phase 1: the brief

**Load:** the user's request (the "spec"). Nothing else.
**Produce:** the `## Brief` section of `plan.md` (shape in `skeleton.md`).
**Keep:** `plan.md` is the reference from now on; later phases read it, not the conversation.

## Step 1: read the spec first
For each question below, check whether the spec already answers it. If it does, write the answer in the
brief and do not ask. Ask only what is missing.

## Step 2: ask what is missing, one question at a time
Give the question and its proposed answers, plus "or tell me your own" and "choose for me". If the user
says "choose for me", take answer A and say so.

1. **Audience.** A) children, 8 to 12 B) teenagers and general public C) adults.
2. **Genre and stakes.** A) adventure with danger, where choices matter (RPG) B) mystery or investigation
   C) relationships and everyday life.
3. **The promise.** What will the player want to find out or decide? A) a dilemma between two loyalties
   B) a secret to uncover C) a bond to build or a person to win over.
4. **The player.** Name and role. A) a newcomer who knows nothing B) an expert hired for one job C) an
   ordinary person pulled into events.
5. **Scale.** A) short: 3 steps B) medium: 5 steps C) long: 8 steps. (One step = one file.)
6. **Cast.** A) 2 NPCs B) 3 NPCs C) 4 NPCs. More than 4 makes a small model slow and confused.
7. **Limits.** What must not appear. A) nothing special B) gentle: no gore, no romance C) the user lists them.
8. **Language** (ask this one only if the spec does not say). A) English (recommended: small models write and
   play better in it, and the player can switch the game to French) B) French, only if the story will surely
   be played in French C) another language.

If two answers fit (a newcomer who is also an ordinary person), take the first one that fits and say so.

## Step 3: write the brief
Write each answer as one line in `plan.md` under `## Brief`. Add one line "Why it is interesting:" that
restates the promise (question 3) in your own words, from the audience's point of view.

## Checklist
- Every one of the eight lines is filled, and none is longer than one sentence.
- The user has read the brief and said it is right.
