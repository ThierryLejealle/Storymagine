# Phase 2: the plan (cast and steps on one page)

**Load:** `plan.md` (the brief) and the shapes of the two tables in `skeleton.md`.
**Produce:** the `## Cast` and `## Steps` tables in `plan.md`. Together with the brief they fit on one page.
**Keep:** the tables are the plan; later phases read them, not your reasoning.

## Step 1: sketch the cast
One row per NPC, as many as the brief says (2 to 4). Fill every cell with one short phrase:
- **Role** in the story (who they are to the player).
- **Wants** (what they try to get during the story).
- **Secret** (one line: what they hide, which the player can discover).
- **Voice** (how they speak: two things you could hear, not an adjective. "short sentences, jokes when
  nervous", not "funny").

Make the NPCs different from each other in role, want and voice. Give each a reason to meet the player.

## Step 1b: for a mystery, fix the answer
Write one line under the brief: what is the truth (who, what, how). Then check that every clue in the steps
points to it, and that no clue gives it away before the step that needs it.

## Step 2: lay out the steps
One row per step, as many as the brief says. A step is a real stage of the story, not a single scene. Fill:
- **Place** and **Who is there**: if some NPCs only join later, note it ({npc-arrives}/{npc-leaves}).
- **Starting point**: one concrete fact that opens the step.
- **What each NPC does in this step**: for each NPC present, what it does or tries during THIS step, in one
  short phrase ("Nell asks who the letter is for"). Not the outcome of the step and nothing from later
  steps: the plan is for you, but these phrases become the lines the NPCs read.
- **NEXT ACT fact**: ONE observable fact that ends the step ("the door is open", "Ione has named the
  thief"). Not a feeling, not two facts.
- **Character changes**: what changes about an NPC during this step ({npc-delta}, or a `## tag` to switch on).
- **Different from previous by**: if you cannot fill this cell, two steps are too alike: merge or change one.

Place a reveal in the step where it is first useful, not earlier. The last step ends the story: it has no
NEXT ACT fact, put "end" there.

## Step 3: approval
Show the brief and the tables to the user together. Change what they ask. Continue only after a yes.

## Checklist
- Cast: 2 to 4 rows, all cells filled, voices clearly different.
- Steps: as many rows as the brief says, each NEXT ACT fact is one observable fact, no empty
  "Different from previous by" cell.
- The promise of the brief is visible in at least two steps.
